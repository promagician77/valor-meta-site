    What the audit found

      The audit

      Every campaign and angle is judged against two numbers worked out from the cash a client actually pays: the break-even CAC, and a target CAC that returns 1.5 times what it cost. Where a campaign has fewer than 8 clients, it is judged on cost per qualified lead instead, because a CAC built on 3 clients is mostly noise.

        Creative testing: 20 variations a week

        The arithmetic first. Twenty variations share the test budget, so each one buys a few clicks' worth of judgement, not a client's. The system below judges each stage on the data that stage can actually produce.

        This week's 20, mostly built on what already works

        How winners are picked, and how often each way is right

        2,000 simulated weeks of 20 variations. In each, two ads are promoted, and the simulation knows each ad's true cost per client. "Beat the control" means the promoted ad really costs less per client than the current best ad.

          Pre-flight copy check

          Meta rejects copy that implies a viewer's financial status, and regulators have pursued tax-relief firms over "pennies on the dollar" and impersonation claims. Every variation goes through this before launch. It flags copy for review; it is not legal advice.

            From GoHighLevel stage to Meta conversion

            Meta can only optimize for what it is told. When a lead moves stage in GoHighLevel, a webhook sends one Conversions API event: instant-form leads use Meta's Conversion Leads format with the lead ID; website leads reuse the pixel's event ID so Meta counts them once, and later stages carry fbc and hashed contact details. The receiver is in the kit and tested against a fake Meta endpoint.
